Why this tab exists
HHC has made specific, public, verifiable commitments on equity, sustainability, and community health. Every gap between stated goal and current condition is a design obligation — not just a design opportunity. All HHC goal statements are verified from primary sources dated below.
HHC + P&W stated goals → community reality → design alignment
HHC stated goal verified
Carbon neutral by 2050 / 50% emissions reduction by 2030
Health Sector Climate Pledge signed April 19, 2024. Scope 3 inventory required within 1 year. Executive-level lead designated. Climate resilience plan required. HHS recognition Dec 2023. HHC joins 15% of US hospitals that have made this commitment. CEO Flaks: "important recognition of the nexus between environmental health and public health."
HHC PR Newswire Apr 19 2024 · HHS Health Sector Climate Pledge · Hartford Business Journal Apr 2024
Community reality verified
Tension
The $1B campus expansion adds embodied carbon not covered by Scope 1/2 targets. I-84 diesel corridor runs directly adjacent — contributing to asthma rates in the top 10% nationally. The built environment is both a carbon source and a community health receptor. Operational carbon commitments do not address embodied carbon in the 5 new buildings.
CDC PLACES 2024 · HHC Master Plan Hartford Planning Commission 2024
Design alignment
Low-embodied carbon structure strategy for all 5 new buildings — benchmarked against P&W 2030 Commitment targets. MERV-13+ filtration addressing diesel corridor exposure. Renewable-ready MEP. Green roof and bioswale for air quality and runoff. The pledge creates a design mandate: the Climate Pledge's community health framing makes the asthma/envelope performance connection explicit.
HHC stated goal verified
Center for Equity — "zip code cannot define life expectancy"
HHC launched its Center for Equity October 2023 with Chief Equity Officer Sara Lewis. Goal: break down barriers to care — language, race, geography. CEO Jeffrey Flaks at launch: "Where someone is born, their zip code cannot define their life expectancy." Dedicated Health Equity Department and DEIB Council.
HHC Center for Equity launch · WTNH Oct 2023 · HHC Careers DEIB page
Community reality verified
Critical gap
NE Hartford life expectancy: 68.9 years (94% Black + Hispanic). Westport: 89.1 years (91% white). Same state. 20-year gap. The campus is geographically adjacent to communities experiencing this gap — yet dental access (52% vs CT 68%), PCP rates (52% of Hispanic enrollees unattached), and preventive screening lag the state. The gap is documented, persistent, and immediately adjacent.
Access Health CT · CDC USALEEP 2010-2015 · UConn HDI / AJMC 2023
Design alignment
The campus expansion's Asylum Ave ground floor is the physical manifestation of this stated goal. Walk-in primary care without appointment. Dental access without hospital navigation. Financial counseling at entry. Spanish-English wayfinding as standard, not retrofit. A campus that reads as a parking structure from Asylum Ave directly contradicts the CEO's public commitment.
HHC stated goal verified
UConn / HHC Health Equity Clinic — address legal barriers to health
Partnership with UConn School of Law launched January 2025 at Hartford Hospital. Law students work with medical providers in primary care clinics to identify legal barriers — housing, income insecurity, disability discrimination — that directly impact clinical outcomes. First clinic of its kind in Connecticut.
UConn Today Dec 2024 · HHC partnership announcement Jan 2025
Community reality verified
Active tension
The clinic exists because legal barriers are health barriers. An uninsured ALICE household faces: ALS ambulance bill $1,323 (CT OEMS 2024), housing cost burden ~54% (DataHaven est.), Medicaid projected to cover 100K+ fewer CT residents by 2034. These are the exact legal-health intersections the clinic addresses — the campus physical design must not create new barriers that undermine this program.
CT OEMS 2024 · DataHaven 2023 · HHC CHNA 2025 · KFF/CBO
Design alignment
Co-locate the Health Equity Clinic intake with the community-facing ground floor — not inside hospital navigation. Financial counseling and legal navigation visible and accessible from the street. The building should help untangle compounding legal and health needs. Design for patients who arrive in crisis — not just patients who have already navigated the system.
HHC stated goal verified
Rooftop garden · food waste reduction · reusable devices and linens
HHC already operates a "large and productive" rooftop garden, has begun food waste tracking and reduction, and shifted to reusable devices and linens. These existing programs were cited as precedent by CEO Flaks at the Health Sector Climate Pledge signing, April 2024. Active, operational programs — not aspirational.
HHC PR Newswire Apr 19 2024 — Flaks direct quote
Community reality verified
Missed connection
Asylum Hill and Frog Hollow are food-stressed neighborhoods adjacent to a campus that produces food and has active food programming — but that programming is internal, not community-facing. Diabetes at 18.4% (+70% vs CT) and obesity at 36.2% (+32%) are directly linked to food access. The campus has the infrastructure but the physical design doesn't connect it to community need.
CDC PLACES 2024 · DataHaven neighborhood analysis
Design alignment
Extend the rooftop food program to a SNAP-accessible community distribution point at the ground-floor Asylum Ave edge. Connect food waste reduction to a community kitchen or produce access point. This extends existing HHC operations outward — no new program required, just a physical design connection between internal infrastructure and external need at the campus boundary.
Perkins & Will goal verified
2030 Commitment — net zero operational carbon, embodied carbon reduction
P&W committed all projects to net zero operational carbon by 2030 and active embodied carbon reduction. The PRECEDE tool is a P&W research output — built to embed public health data into design decisions at schematic design phase. This dashboard is that tool applied to the Hartford campus context.
P&W 2030 Commitment · PRECEDE launch Nov 2023 · P&W Human Experience Lab
Project reality
Active design brief
The HHC 5-building expansion includes a 600,000 sf garage/clinic building and new cancer center — high-embodied-carbon typologies. P&W's 2030 Commitment requires active embodied carbon benchmarking from schematic design. The community health data in this dashboard — particularly asthma burden from I-84 — directly links the P&W sustainability target to community health outcomes. This is the PRECEDE-to-design chain.
HHC Master Plan / Hartford Business Journal Oct 2024 · P&W 2030 Commitment
Design alignment
Embodied carbon benchmarking using EC3 or Tally from schematic design. Low-carbon structure options analysis (mass timber, high fly-ash concrete). Adaptive reuse of existing campus buildings first. Renewable-ready MEP. High-performance envelope to address diesel corridor asthma burden — making the envelope a public health intervention and a carbon intervention simultaneously. PRECEDE data supports both arguments to the client.

Design premise
The standard hospital brief asks how to make care efficient for patients who arrive. The Hartford campus brief must also ask: why are people not arriving — and what can the building do about that? Each response is tied to a verified health indicator and a specific HHC stated goal.
Architectural
Respiratory-first envelope
13 Hartford tracts rank in top 10% nationally for asthma. I-84 diesel corridor is directly adjacent. MERV-13+ filtration, no off-gassing materials, operable windows on patient-facing facades. Low-VOC throughout. Advances HHC Climate Pledge — the environmental-public health nexus the pledge explicitly names.
Driver: Asthma 16.8% (+65%) · COPD · I-84 diesel · HHC Climate Pledge Apr 2024
Architectural
Community-facing ground floor
New HHC garage/clinic building — activate Asylum Ave edge with dental, metabolic health, behavioral health walk-in, interpreter-supported primary care. Built form of HHC Center for Equity commitment. 52% of Hispanic patients have no PCP. This floor directly addresses that gap.
Driver: 52% Hispanic no PCP · dental 52% vs CT 68% · HHC Center for Equity 2023
Interior
Circadian + biophilic environments
Depression and poor mental health 38-43% above state average. Tunable LED 2700-6500K. Maximum daylight in waiting and recovery zones. Biophilic elements throughout. Eliminate uniform fluorescent. PRECEDE recommends this for any site with elevated MH burden — Hartford qualifies on multiple indicators.
Driver: Depression 32.2% · Poor MH 23.1% · ALICE economic stress · housing instability
Interior
De-stigmatized behavioral health access
Walk-in behavioral health at primary care check-in — not a separate hallway. Acoustic privacy without isolation. Wayfinding in Spanish and English as the standard, not a retrofit. Hartford has 50%+ of CT's highest-burden poor mental health census tracts. Supports HHC Health Equity Clinic co-location model (Jan 2025).
Driver: Depression + MH · 50%+ state worst MH tracts · HHC Health Equity Clinic 2025
Urban
Transit-legible campus edges
~38% of Hartford households have no vehicle (ACS estimate). CTfastrak and Route 60 are primary access routes. Seymour St and Asylum Ave edges must be pedestrian-prioritized, transit-shelter integrated, active at all hours. A campus that reads as parking infrastructure from the street contradicts HHC's stated access commitment.
Driver: ~38% no-vehicle (est.) · missed appts 2x state avg · CTfastrak · ALICE no rideshare margin
Urban
Active mobility + visible stairs
Obesity (36.2%) and physical inactivity (35.1%) are structural outcomes of car-dependent neighborhoods — not individual choice. Prominently located, naturally lit stairways as default movement paths. Grade-separated pedestrian connections to Asylum Hill and Frog Hollow. Morrow High School (Atlanta, P&W) precedent: active stair design measurably reduced sedentary behavior.
Driver: Obesity +32% · Physical inactivity +57% · diabetes management · P&W 2030 Commitment
Urban
Ground-floor food access
HHC already operates a rooftop garden and food waste reduction program. Extend to SNAP-accessible produce distribution at Asylum Ave ground floor. No new program required — just a physical design connection between existing internal HHC infrastructure and the food-stressed community immediately adjacent. SDOH intervention for diabetes and obesity.
Driver: Diabetes 18.4% · Obesity 36.2% · HHC rooftop garden (existing) · food desert adjacency
Architectural
Interpreter infrastructure
44.5% of Hartford is Hispanic; Frog Hollow and Clay Arsenal are Spanish-primary. Interpreter rooms and video interpretation at registration, triage, and discharge. Bilingual signage as the standard throughout. Language access is a clinical safety requirement — the HHC DEIB commitment requires this to be built in from schematic design, not added as a post-occupancy retrofit.
Driver: 44.5% Hispanic · 23.7% foreign-born · 52% no PCP · HHC DEIB Council
Interior
Staff resilience spaces
Campus serving a high-acuity, high-trauma-burden population with disproportionate chronic disease and MH load generates significant caregiver stress. Acoustically private staff respite with daylight and biophilic conditions is retention infrastructure — not luxury. Directly supports the workforce wellbeing goals in HHC CHNA 2025.
Driver: High-acuity population · MH burden · workforce retention · HHC CHNA 2025
