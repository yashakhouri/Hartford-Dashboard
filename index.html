Insurance literacy gap — CT enrollees without a PCP by race
Having insurance does not mean using it. Low health insurance literacy — particularly around how to find and use a PCP — disproportionately affects Black and Hispanic enrollees in CT. Without a PCP, every health event routes through the ER.
Ambulance cost exposure — CT rates 2024
Ground ambulances are excluded from the No Surprises Act. An uninsured Hartford resident calling 911 for an ALS-level emergency receives a bill of $1,323+. For an ALICE household earning $35K–45K, this represents 3–4 weeks of net pay. 50% of insured ambulance patients are still at risk of a surprise out-of-network bill.

Life expectancy — the design brief
Life expectancy in northeast Hartford tracts (94% Black and Hispanic) is 68.9 years. Life expectancy in Westport (91% white) is 89.1 years. That 20-year gap exists within the same small state. The design brief for the HHC campus expansion is not just clinical — it is a spatial justice intervention.

Housing instability → health outcomes

A resident in the most unstable housing tract is 34–36% more likely to report poor mental or physical health. The correlation is direct, not incidental.

The highest-instability neighborhoods — Asylum Hill, Clay Arsenal, Northeast, Upper Albany — directly ring the HHC campus. Smoking, COPD, diabetes, obesity, and physical inactivity all track housing instability at the census-tract level.

This means the campus expansion's ground-floor program strategy is not a nice-to-have. It is the clinical intervention.

Transit access to Hartford Hospital

~38% of Hartford households have no vehicle. CTfastrak and CTtransit Route 60 (Farmington Ave / Asylum Ave) are the primary access corridors into the campus for Asylum Hill and Frog Hollow residents.

The Seymour St / Farmington Ave intersection is the critical node. Bus frequency drops off after 7pm and on weekends — exactly when non-emergency conditions become emergencies.

Design implication: campus edges must be transit-legible, pedestrian-prioritized, and active at all hours — not a sea of surface parking between community and care.

Indicators that don't make it into standard CHNAs
These are the factors that determine whether a person accesses care at all — before any clinical outcome is recorded. They are structural, financial, and cultural. Standard health assessments measure what happens inside the system. These measure what stops people from getting there.

PRECEDE Act layer — evidence-based design responses
Each response is tied directly to a documented health indicator for the campus context. Scale: Architectural (building skin, systems, program) · Interior (spatial experience, materials, environment) · Urban (site, street, campus edge, mobility).
Design premise
The standard hospital design brief asks: how do we make care efficient and comfortable for patients who arrive? The Hartford campus brief must also ask: why are people not arriving — and what can the building do about that? Ground floor activation, transit legibility, interpreter infrastructure, and walk-in access points are not amenities. They are public health infrastructure.
Architectural
Respiratory-first envelope
13 Hartford census tracts rank in the top 10% nationally for asthma rates. The I-84 corridor runs directly adjacent to the campus — diesel particulate exposure is continuous for Asylum Hill. Specify MERV-13+ filtration, no off-gassing materials, operable windows on patient-facing facades. Low-VOC finishes throughout. Asthma management begins at the building skin.
Driver: Asthma 16.8% (CT avg 10.2%) · COPD · I-84 diesel exposure · top national decile
Architectural
Ground-floor community health interface
The new HHC garage / clinic building is a design intervention opportunity at the Asylum Ave campus edge. Ground floor should activate with dental, metabolic health, behavioral health walk-in, and interpreter-supported primary care. This is the interface between the hospital and the community that cannot reach it. 52% of Hispanic patients have no PCP — this floor can address that directly.
Driver: 52% Hispanic + 46% Black enrollees no PCP · dental visits 52% (CT avg 68%) · Asylum Hill adjacency
Interior
Circadian + biophilic environments
Depression and poor mental health are 38–43% above state average. Mental health issues in Hartford span the full city — not concentrated in one neighborhood — suggesting systemic stress load. Tunable LED lighting (2700–6500K), maximum daylight penetration into waiting and recovery zones, biophilic elements in corridors and patient rooms. Eliminate uniform fluorescent environments.
Driver: Depression 32.2% · Poor MH 23.1% (CT avg 16.2%) · housing stress · ALICE economic pressure
Interior
Integrated behavioral health access
De-stigmatized walk-in behavioral health points co-located at primary care check-in — not down a separate hallway. Design for acoustic privacy without spatial isolation. Seating that supports different cultural norms of personal space. Wayfinding in Spanish and English at minimum. Hartford has 50%+ of the state's highest-burden poor mental health census tracts.
Driver: Depression + MH · 50%+ state's worst MH tracts · language access · NE Hartford life expectancy 68.9yr
Urban
Transit-legible campus edges
38% of Hartford households have no vehicle. CTfastrak and Route 60 (Farmington/Asylum) are how most community members reach the campus. The Seymour St and Asylum Ave edges must be pedestrian-prioritized, transit-shelter integrated, and active at all hours. Wayfinding from Asylum Hill residential clusters to campus entrances must be explicit. A campus that reads as parking infrastructure from the street fails its population.
Driver: 38% no-vehicle households · missed appts 2× state avg · CTfastrak access · ALICE no-margin for taxi/rideshare
Urban
Active mobility + visible stairs
Obesity (36.2%) and physical inactivity (35.1%) are structural outcomes of car-dependent neighborhoods and sedentary environments, not individual choice. New buildings should have prominently located, naturally lit stairways as the default path — not the emergency exit. Grade-separated pedestrian paths connecting to Asylum Hill and Frog Hollow. End-of-trip cycling facilities.
Driver: Obesity +32% · Physical inactivity +57% · Morrow High School active design precedent · diabetes management
Urban
Food access + green infrastructure
Asylum Hill and Frog Hollow are food-stressed neighborhoods. The campus produces significant food waste. Ground-floor or rooftop food programming — community kitchen, produce distribution point, or SNAP-accessible café — addresses a direct SDOH driver for diabetes and obesity while activating campus edges. Green infrastructure (bioswales, street trees) reduces heat island and air quality burden.
Driver: Diabetes 18.4% · Obesity 36.2% · ALICE households · food desert proximity · air quality / asthma
Architectural
Interpreter infrastructure — spatial + digital
44.5% of Hartford is Hispanic; Frog Hollow and Clay Arsenal are Spanish-primary communities. Language access is a clinical safety requirement, not a courtesy. Design for interpreter rooms and video interpretation stations at registration, triage, and discharge. Signage in Spanish and English at minimum. Low health insurance literacy among Spanish-speaking populations requires face-to-face navigation support at campus entry points.
Driver: 44.5% Hispanic · 23.7% foreign-born · 52% no PCP · health insurance literacy gap · AJMC CT data
Interior
Staff resilience + respite spaces
A campus serving a high-acuity, high-complexity patient population with disproportionate trauma and chronic disease burden generates significant caregiver stress. Dedicated staff respite areas — acoustically private, with daylight and biophilic conditions — are not luxury items. They are retention infrastructure. Hartford's healthcare workforce is already strained; the built environment can reduce burnout load.
Driver: High-acuity population · MH burden · workforce retention · HHC 2025 CHNA staff wellbeing findings
